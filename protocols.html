Smart home protocols

How Zigbee, Wi-Fi, Matter, Z-Wave, Thread and Bluetooth differ, which one to pick when buying new devices for Home Assistant, and what extra hardware you need.

The short version

Five of these are radios: the way a device physically talks. Matter is different: it's a common language that runs on top of Wi-Fi, Thread or Ethernet, so any Matter device works with any Matter controller (Home Assistant, Apple, Google, Amazon).

  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are the radios your phone already has. Home Assistant reaches Wi-Fi devices through your router.
  • Zigbee, Thread and Z-Wave are low-power radios made for smart homes. Their devices form a mesh: plugged-in devices pass messages along, so the network gets stronger as you add more. Home Assistant needs a small USB radio (or a built-in one) to talk to them.
Not on the Windows/Docker setupZigbee, Z-Wave, Thread and Bluetooth all need a USB radio, and Docker Desktop on Windows can't pass USB devices through. To use them, run Home Assistant on its own device (see Extra hardware).

Wi-Fi

Speed10s–100s Mbit/s
Range30–50 m from the router
PowerNeeds mains power

Used for: cameras, video doorbells, smart plugs and relays (Shelly), speakers (Sonos, Google Cast), TVs, robot vacuums, and DIY sensors built with ESPHome.

How it differs: it's the only option fast enough for video, and needs no extra hardware. But it uses far too much power for small battery sensors, and every device is one more client on your router; with 30–50+ devices a cheap router can struggle. Many Wi-Fi devices also depend on the maker's cloud.

When to use: for things that are plugged in and either need bandwidth (cameras) or have a local integration in Home Assistant: Shelly, ESPHome, WLED, Sonos, Google Cast. Avoid cloud-only brands where you can; if their servers go down, so does your automation.

Zigbee

Speed250 kbit/s
Range10–20 m per hop
PowerCoin cell lasts years

Used for: motion, door/window, temperature and leak sensors, buttons and remotes, bulbs, plugs, blinds. IKEA, Aqara, Philips Hue, Sonoff, Tuya Zigbee and many more.

How it differs: a low-power mesh. Every plugged-in device (bulb, plug) is a router that relays messages for others, so range grows with every device you add. Battery devices are end devices that sleep most of the time. It's the biggest and cheapest smart home ecosystem, and Home Assistant supports it very well through the built-in ZHA integration or Zigbee2MQTT.

When to use: the default for battery sensors and buttons, and a great choice for lights and plugs. Brands sometimes stretch the standard, so check a device is supported before buying (the Zigbee2MQTT device list is a good reference).

Shares 2.4 GHz with Wi-FiZigbee and Wi-Fi can interfere. Put the Zigbee radio on a USB extension cable away from the computer, and pick a Zigbee channel that doesn't overlap your Wi-Fi (Zigbee 15, 20 or 25 usually work well).

Z-Wave

Speed9.6–100 kbit/s
Range30–40 m per hop; LR up to ~1 km
PowerCoin cell lasts years

Used for: door locks, wall switches and dimmers, thermostats, sirens, garage doors, and sensors in larger houses or outbuildings.

How it differs: it runs below 1 GHz, so it doesn't fight Wi-Fi and goes through walls better than the 2.4 GHz radios. Every device is certified, so different brands work together very reliably. Z-Wave Long Range devices talk directly to the hub over long distances, which is useful for sheds, gates and gardens. The downsides are higher prices and a smaller choice, especially in Europe. Home Assistant uses the Z-Wave JS integration.

When to use: when reliability matters most (locks, alarms), in big houses with thick walls, or when you need range outdoors.

Frequencies are regionalA US Z-Wave device does not work on a European network, and the other way round. Buy the EU version (868 MHz) of both the USB radio and the devices if you live in Europe.

Thread

Speed250 kbit/s
Range10–20 m per hop
PowerCoin cell lasts years

Used for: almost always as the radio underneath Matter: sensors, plugs, bulbs, blinds, locks from Eve, Nanoleaf, IKEA (new range), Aqara and others.

How it differs: technically a close cousin of Zigbee (same radio chip, same speed), but it speaks normal internet protocol (IPv6). A border router connects the Thread mesh to your home network. Border routers from different brands (Home Assistant, Apple TV/HomePod, Google Nest Hub, some Amazon Echos) can join the same Thread network, so you're not tied to one hub.

When to use: you don't really choose Thread on its own; you choose a Matter over Thread device. To make Home Assistant a border router you need a radio flashed with Thread firmware (see Extra hardware). Docs: Thread integration.

Matter not a radio

Runs overWi-Fi, Thread, Ethernet
SetupQR code, via Bluetooth
ControlAlways local

Used for: plugs, bulbs, switches, sensors, blinds, locks, thermostats, robot vacuums. More device types are added every release.

How it differs: Matter is a shared standard backed by Apple, Google, Amazon, Samsung and others. A Matter device is controlled locally, without the maker's cloud, and it can be in several systems at once (multi-admin), e.g. Home Assistant and Apple Home together. You add it by scanning its QR code with the Home Assistant phone app; the phone uses Bluetooth for the first setup only. Home Assistant uses the Matter integration.

When to use: a good default for new devices: if a product has a Matter logo, it will work with Home Assistant and keep working if you ever change systems. Prefer Matter over Thread for battery devices, and Matter over Wi-Fi only for plugged-in ones.

Matter needs IPv6 and a flat networkMatter relies on IPv6 and multicast on your home network. Keep Home Assistant and your devices on the same network/VLAN, and don't disable IPv6 on your router.

Bluetooth (BLE)

Speed1–2 Mbit/s
Range5–10 m indoors
PowerCoin cell lasts months–years

Used for: cheap thermometers (Xiaomi, Govee, Inkbird), plant sensors, SwitchBot devices, some locks, presence detection (is my phone or watch home?), and Matter's first-time setup.

How it differs: short range and no mesh. To cover a whole house, use Bluetooth proxies: small ESP32 boards (around €10) that listen for Bluetooth nearby and forward it to Home Assistant over Wi-Fi or Ethernet. Home Assistant uses the Bluetooth integration.

When to use: for specific devices that only come in Bluetooth, especially cheap temperature and humidity sensors. Not for lights or switches you rely on every day.

Buying new devices for Home Assistant

There's no single winner. Most good setups mix two or three. This order works well:

DeviceBest choiceWhy
Battery sensors, buttonsZigbee, or Matter over ThreadYears on a coin cell, cheap, huge choice (Zigbee) or future-proof (Thread)
Bulbs, plugs, switchesZigbee or Matter over ThreadThey also strengthen the mesh for your sensors
Relays behind switchesWi-Fi with local control (Shelly, ESPHome)Cheap, fast, no cloud; or Zigbee/Z-Wave versions
Door locksZ-Wave or Matter over ThreadReliability and security matter most
Cameras, doorbellsWi-Fi or EthernetThe only options with enough bandwidth
Outbuildings, gardens, gatesZ-Wave Long Range, or Wi-Fi/EthernetRange
Cheap thermometersBluetooth + an ESPHome proxyA few euros each
Our recommendation
  • Start with Zigbee as your main low-power network. It's the cheapest, has the most devices, and Home Assistant supports it best today.
  • Prefer Matter (over Thread) when a device you want offers it. It's local, works with every big platform, and is where the industry is going.
  • Use Wi-Fi only for plugged-in devices with a local integration, and for cameras.
  • Add Z-Wave only if you need its range or reliability, or already own Z-Wave devices.

Before buying any device, check

  • Does it work without the maker's cloud? Look it up in the Home Assistant integrations list. “Local Push” or “Local Polling” is what you want.
  • Does it have the Works with Home Assistant badge? Those are tested by the Home Assistant team.
  • For Z-Wave: is it the right frequency for your country?
  • For a “Matter” device: is it Matter over Thread or over Wi-Fi? The box usually says.

Extra hardware you may need

Wi-Fi and Matter-over-Wi-Fi devices need nothing extra. For the others you need a radio. These are the official Home Assistant ones; they're set up automatically and get firmware updates through Home Assistant:

Zigbee or ThreadHome Assistant Connect ZBT-2~$49. Replaces the older ZBT-1.
Z-WaveHome Assistant Connect ZWA-2~$69. Supports Z-Wave Long Range. Choose your region.
BluetoothESPHome Bluetooth proxy (any ESP32 board, e.g. M5Stack Atom Lite, or a ready-made PoE board)~€10–30 each. Install from the browser, no coding.
Any USB radioUSB 2.0 extension cable, 0.5–1 m~€5. Keeps the radio away from USB 3 and computer noise. Really helps.
One radio = Zigbee or ThreadThe ZBT-2 runs one of the two at a time. If you want both Zigbee and Thread, buy two of them (one flashed for each), or use an Apple TV, HomePod or Google Nest Hub you already have as the Thread border router.

Home Assistant Green

Home Assistant Green has Ethernet and two USB ports, but no smart home radios, no Bluetooth and no Wi-Fi. (It doesn't need Wi-Fi to control Wi-Fi devices; it reaches them through your router.)

  • Zigbee: ZBT-2 + USB extension cable
  • Thread / Matter over Thread: a second ZBT-2 with Thread firmware, or an existing Apple/Google border router
  • Z-Wave: ZWA-2
  • Bluetooth: one or more ESPHome Bluetooth proxies

Home Assistant Yellow

Home Assistant Yellow already has a built-in Zigbee/Thread radio (one or the other, like the ZBT-2). Production ended in October 2025; it's still supported, but new buyers should get a Green or a mini PC instead.

  • Zigbee or Thread: built in. For both, add a ZBT-2 for the other one.
  • Z-Wave: ZWA-2
  • Bluetooth: built in only if your Compute Module has wireless (CM4/CM5 “W” versions). Otherwise, or for better coverage, ESPHome Bluetooth proxies.

A mini PC, e.g. Dell OptiPlex 3040 Micro (i5-6400T, 8 GB, 240 GB SSD)

A great choice: far faster than a Green, and second-hand ones are cheap. 8 GB RAM and a 240 GB SSD are plenty. It has no smart home radios, and Bluetooth is usually missing or unreliable, so plan on the same extras as the Green:

  • Zigbee and/or Thread: ZBT-2 (two for both) + USB extension cable
  • Z-Wave: ZWA-2
  • Bluetooth: ESPHome Bluetooth proxies
Replace Windows with Home Assistant OSDon't keep Windows 11 and run Home Assistant in Docker Desktop on it: that's the setup on the Windows page, and it can't use USB radios. Instead install Home Assistant OS directly on the SSD (this erases Windows), following the Generic x86-64 guide. In the BIOS, turn off Secure Boot and set After Power Loss to Power On so it restarts by itself after a power cut. Then restore your backup from the Windows trial.
